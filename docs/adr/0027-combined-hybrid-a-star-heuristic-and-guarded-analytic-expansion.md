# ADR-0027: Combined Hybrid A* heuristic and guarded near-goal analytic expansion

**Status:** Accepted (dual-component performance default; numerical/cache/heuristic guarantee details open)  
**Date:** 2026-10-25  
**Updates:** [ADR-0015](0015-admissible-default-heuristics.md), [ADR-0025](0025-approved-ackermann-mvp-settings-and-primitive-sample-collision.md), [ADR-0026](0026-reeds-shepp-near-goal-analytic-expansion.md)

## Context

Euclidean distance to the goal region alone is too weak for efficient vehicle Hybrid A* search in many obstacle maps. Stanford Junior and Nav2 Smac Hybrid use complementary estimates for vehicle turning constraints and obstacle detours. We also need to avoid accepting a lengthy/costly Reeds–Shepp shortcut merely because its start has a small Euclidean distance to the goal.

## Decision

1. **Make two-component heuristic part of the first Ackermann MVP.** Implement **(a) nonholonomic, obstacle-free** distance based on the minimum-turn-radius forward/reverse Reeds–Shepp geometry and **(b) holonomic, obstacle-aware** 2D goal-directed cost-to-go field obtained by reverse Dijkstra/dynamic programming on the current request's static occupancy grid. The search combines them as `h = max(h_kinematic, h_obstacle)`, with a separately validated cheaper Euclidean/zero fallback. Preserve model compatibility: RS is specific to suitable Ackermann states and must not be imposed on unrelated models.
2. **Respect goal tolerance, cost units and admissibility.** Our goal accepts a 0.25 m / 10-degree region, not necessarily the exact requested pose. Plain RS distance to the **goal center** can *overestimate* the distance to the accepted goal region. Likewise, ordinary 4/8-connected grid Dijkstra distances are not automatically lower bounds on continuous free-space travel. Do not claim the combined heuristic certified admissible until **each component** has been proven a lower bound for the *configured* equivalent-meter cost and the whole goal region (ADR-0015). **The first Ackermann search uses the two-component performance-oriented / unproven-admissibility heuristic by default**; this deliberately refines ADR-0015's earlier certified-mode-default direction. Maintain an **explicit** certified alternative with Euclidean-to-goal-region or zero fallback. These modes must not silently report optimality; quantized one-arrival-per-key search remains approximate regardless.
3. **Reuse/precompute expensive data.** **Required first-implementation algorithm: lazy, cached, multi-source reverse Dijkstra.** Create/reuse a goal-seeded 2D priority queue and contiguous distances/settlement statuses keyed by immutable map source/revision/grid transform, occupancy/unknown policy, **effective traversable XY goal-region seed set**, connectivity/corner rule and graph edge costs. Seed all free cells whose squares intersect the configured XY goal-tolerance disk. **Do not eagerly settle the whole field**: on a query, resume the retained queue only until the requested cell is settled, the frontier is exhausted or a finite work/time budget is reached. Cache both settled distances and the remaining unsettled frontier for compatible later queries/replans; bound retention/eviction and avoid D*/incremental map-revision repair (ADR-0019). For the kinematic component, use the OMPL-backed RS distance-only evaluator and **an optional bounded precomputed lookup table** over normalized relative goal offsets/heading and turning radius if repeated calls merit it. Do not require per-successor full path construction merely to obtain a heuristic distance; cache only under compatible model/tolerance/discretization assumptions. A lookup must not silently turn an admissible estimate into an overestimate.
4. **Retain near-goal Reeds–Shepp attempts, but guard their quality.** A configurable XY-radius near-goal trigger still governs attempt frequency; the approved configurable trigger is **20.0 m**. Before accepting a candidate, examine **actual Reeds–Shepp geometric length, fully weighted active-MVP cost** (forward/reverse and all gear changes; **no clearance integral**), curvature/model feasibility and whole-body collision result. Add configurable **maximum analytic path length and/or weighted cost/quality threshold**, with only **explicitly configured, reported** exceptions/relaxation for necessary close-quarters maneuvers rather than a hidden bypass of the guard. Rejecting an analytic candidate must not reject the request: normal Hybrid A* continues and the pose-goal region can still be reached by primitives.
5. **No unsupported cost-minimality claim.** OMPL's shortest-geometric-length RS connection is not automatically the least costly under reverse/switch penalties (or an optional future clearance penalty). Feasible-first acceptance may remain the MVP strategy **only after** the guards and final validation. More sophisticated incumbent-comparison/termination policies are deferred to OQ-011 and should be measured against post-optimization path quality.

## Implementation tests

- Construct obstacle-wall detour and heading-opposition fixtures; compare Euclidean-only, nonholonomic-only, obstacle-only, and combined expansions, memory/latency and final validated path quality.
- Test analytic RS distance against exact/near goal and a nonzero goal region. Flag unjustified center-distance admissibility. Test grid 4/8-neighbor overestimation on a tiny free-space map and separate strict/uncertified modes.
- **Goal-region aware seeding:** seed the holonomic reverse-Dijkstra from **all free map cells whose square intersects the accepted XY goal region** (rather than solely the possibly blocked nominal goal-center cell). Apply the request's unknown/threshold policy and map-boundary rules. This is a 2D location-only relaxation; evaluate goal-heading tolerance and complete footprint collision **only at actual continuous Hybrid A* terminal states**. If no legitimate seed exists, diagnose that condition but do not fabricate finite grid distances.
- **Unreachable/unsettled field semantics:** store explicit `Unvisited/Unsettled/Settled/Unreachable` (or equivalent) separately from numeric distances. Use the 2D heuristic only when a finite **settled** value is available. Mark a cell **unreachable** only when the entire lazy frontier is exhausted, never merely because the requested cell has not yet been settled. For unsettled/unreachable cells, use the finite RS/Euclidean heuristic fallback and **do not assign `h=+infinity` or discard a search node solely because the relaxed grid graph fails**. A goal-region seed itself need not be a valid goal pose. This is consistent in principle with Nav2's lazy, non-infinite obstacle heuristic fallback, but is a more explicit state contract.
- **Concurrency/budget rule:** the cached lazy field's queue, distances and statuses are **one mutable unit**. Never share them between simultaneous requests without synchronization; an MVP may serialize access per cached field or use separate mutable per-request frontiers. An immutable map snapshot alone does not make the lazy field thread-safe. When the settlement budget expires, return `Unavailable` with a finite RS/Euclidean fallback, and preserve the queue consistently for later reuse.
- **Discrete graph settings:** choose nonnegative 8-neighbor edge lengths in map meters and a deterministic diagonal-corner-cutting policy; key caches and regression tests to the policy. Do not claim the raw grid value is a certified continuous-space lower bound.
- Test lazy reverse Dijkstra under unknown policy, occupancy threshold, map revisions, rotated origins, free goal-region seeds, blocked nominal center, disconnected/unsettled cells, finite budgets, retained frontier reuse, heap correctness and concurrent-request isolation. Do not conflate this goal-rooted field with obstacle-*clearance* data.
- Test excessively long or expensive near-goal connector rejection despite being within XY trigger radius; ordinary search proceeds. Preserve exceptions needed for reversing/turning in a confined space and configuration-driven triggers. Count and time connector attempts.

## Outstanding details

Numerical heuristic lower-bound constructions, weighting policy for intentionally nonadmissible speed mode, precise Dijkstra cell graph/boundary relaxation, and RS cache dimensions remain implementation-facing decisions. The near-goal trigger is **20.0 m**, and actual analytic connection **length/cost acceptance must be configurable and deterministic**; turn-radius-scaled cap defaults still require scenario tuning.
